# Brand Proposal (revised)

Status: revised after learning the site is a **professor's academic site** (research, PhD-related work), not a general magazine. The earlier "Margina / Tandem / Obzor" directions are dropped: an invented publication name would compete with the one asset that matters in academia, the scholar's own name.

## Brand strategy

- **The brand is the person.** Name, title and affiliation are the identity (e.g. "Prof. Dr. Firstname Lastname"). Scholars are found by name in Google Scholar, ORCID, and citations; the site should match exactly how the name appears on publications. Confirm the exact form with the professor.
- **Promise:** rigorous research, explained clearly, in two languages.
- **Audience:** peers and reviewers, PhD students and prospective students, journalists, funders and collaborators, interested general readers.
- **Credibility signals are the design:** affiliation, ORCID, publication list, citations, CV, contact. A visitor must verify who this is within 5 seconds.
- **Optional tagline** (not a brand name): a short line describing the research area, once the field is known.

## Tone of voice

| Is                                  | Is not                    |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Precise, measured, plainly written  | Jargon-dense, gatekeeping |
| Confident, with evidence            | Promotional, hype         |
| Warm toward students and the public | Chatty, slang, emoji      |
| Honest about uncertainty and limits | Overclaiming results      |

**Calibration from his existing site:** his current voice is warmer and more personal than this table suggests (tagline "A scientist knows what others have done. An innovator is discovering it by himself."; a Quotes page; playful About). Keep that. Rule: precise about science, personal about everything else. Name form to use: "Joško Trošelj" (with diacritics; the site must also be found by "Josko Troselj" and "Trošelj J."). See `DISCOVERY.md` section 11.

Two registers, same voice: **scholarly** (publications, abstracts) and **explanatory** (blog posts for a wider audience, plain language, no unexplained acronyms).
Croatian: written natively, not translated. Academic Croatian uses formal "Vi" in address to readers and applicants; the site speaks about the work in neutral third-person style, so "ti/Vi" rarely arises. Recommendation: **Vi** in the few direct-address spots (newsletter, contact), consistent with an academic setting.

## Visual direction

> The palette, type and assets below are superseded by [DESIGN-DIRECTION.md](DESIGN-DIRECTION.md) ("Water level": deep-teal accent, warm paper, hydrograph line motif). Kept for context.

- **Look:** restrained editorial. Lots of white space, strong typographic hierarchy, nothing decorative.
- **Palette:** off-white paper `#FBFAF7`, ink `#17181C`, one deep accent for links and focus (suggest academic blue `#1F4E8C`, or a deep burgundy if the university's colors suggest it). Dark mode supported, same accent lightened. If the university has brand guidelines, align the accent with them.
- **Type:** a serif with full Croatian diacritic support for reading (Source Serif 4 or Literata); a clean sans for UI and metadata (Inter). Tabular figures for citations and years.
- **Imagery:** professional portrait, lab/field/fieldwork photos if available, figures from papers. Alt text required.
- **Layout priorities:** ~65ch reading measure, visible reference/footnote styling, accessible tables, print stylesheet (people print papers and CVs).

## What a scholarly brand needs that a magazine does not

Math notation (LaTeX rendering), footnotes and references, citation export (BibTeX, RIS), DOI links, PDF downloads, ORCID/Google Scholar/ResearchGate links, publication metadata that Google Scholar understands, a printable CV. These are covered in `DISCOVERY.md` section 10.
